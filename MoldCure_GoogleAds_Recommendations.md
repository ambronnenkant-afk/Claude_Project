## SECTION 1: Critical Issues to Fix First

### 1. One Broad Match Keyword Is Running Your Entire Remediation Campaign
`mold removal` (Broad) generated 2,756 impressions, $750.68 in spend, and 9 of your remediation conversions. Every other remediation keyword shows near-zero spend. You have no visibility into what Google is actually matching — it could be DIY queries, competitor names, or out-of-area searches.

**Fix:** Add `mold removal` as Phrase Match and Exact Match in addition to Broad. Pull the Search Terms Report and add irrelevant matches to your negative keyword list.

---

### 2. Your Own Brand Name Is Negated With No Branded Campaign
`[mold cure]` is a campaign-level negative keyword. Anyone searching "MoldCure" directly will not see your ads. There is no branded campaign in the account.

**Fix:** Create a dedicated Branded Campaign targeting:
- `[moldcure]`
- `[mold cure]`
- `"moldcure dc"`
- `"mold cure washington"`

Branded clicks are your cheapest and highest-converting traffic. They are currently going unserved.

---

### 3. Pinned Headlines Are Hurting Ad Strength
- Delaware Testing Ad 1 has 7 headlines all pinned to position 1 — Google cannot test combinations.
- DC Testing Ad 2 has `{KeyWord:Air Quality Testing}` pinned to position 1, rated "Average" strength.

**Fix:** Unpin all headlines you do not have a specific compliance reason to lock. Let Google's RSA system optimize combinations.

---

### 4. Guarantee Language Is Inconsistent
Ads mention a **12-month** guarantee in some headlines and a **2-year warranty** in sitelinks. This creates confusion and undermines trust.

**Fix:** Pick one accurate term and use it everywhere. Note: If your Pure Maintenance franchise agreement allows promoting the up-to-10-year warranty, that would be a significant competitive advantage over all other dry fog operators in your market.

---

### 5. Two Removed Campaigns Have Wrong Brand/Location Copy
Maryland and Virginia Remediation ads contain descriptions referencing "S. Carolina" and "Pure Air Restored" — copy-pasted from another franchise, never corrected.

**Fix:** Correct before reactivating MD or VA.

---

### 6. Delaware Campaign Is Active But Generating Nothing
DE campaign spent ~$28 in the period with 0 conversions.

**Fix:** Either increase DE budget to a competitive level, or pause it and concentrate all spend in DC until DC is fully optimized and profitable enough to expand.

---

### 7. Conversion Tracking Gap on Sitelinks
Your two highest-click sitelinks ("1-Day Mold Treatment" at 23 clicks, "Air & Mold Testing" at 24 clicks) show zero conversions. This is either a tracking gap or a landing page conversion problem.

**Fix:** Audit the conversion tracking pixel on all sitelink destination pages. Ensure the thank-you/confirmation page fires the conversion event.

## SECTION 4: Competitive Differentiation Strategy

### Your Three Competitive Moats

**1. Verified Outcomes**
Every competitor makes claims. Almost none document results with pre/post air quality testing data. "Zero viable spores" backed by actual test numbers — e.g., "Pre-treatment: 2,400 spores/m³ → Post-treatment: 0 viable spores" — is a category-defining differentiator. Make this the centerpiece of your proof page and case studies.

**2. The Disruption Gap**
SERVPRO and AdvantaClean displace families for days, tear out drywall, and leave reconstruction bills. Your same-day, no-demolition treatment attacks the #1 pain point in traditional remediation. Lead with it in every ad and every page.

**3. The Skeptic-Conversion Opportunity**
Traditional remediators (HomeCleanse, Alpha Environmental, etc.) actively publish "why dry fogging doesn't work" articles that rank for dry fogging keywords. Run ads intercepting searches like "does dry fogging work" and "is dry fog mold removal effective" and point them to a science/proof page. Converting a skeptic mid-research is more valuable and more profitable than capturing an already-convinced buyer.

---

### The Counter-Narrative You Must Address
SERVPRO explicitly tells consumers that "mold cannot be 100% removed" — this is designed to undercut dry fog claims. Your counter: clarify that **"zero viable spores"** is the clinically relevant metric. Dead spores don't harm you; viable spores do. Post-treatment air quality tests showing zero viable spores settle the argument empirically regardless of method.

## SECTION 6: Landing Page Best Practices

### Above the Fold (must-have)
- Phone number click-to-call in top right, mobile-optimized

- Short form: Name, phone, zip code — three fields maximum
- "Get Your Free Inspection" CTA button (high-contrast color)
- Page load time under 2 seconds

### Trust Section (fold 2)
- Before/after air quality test results with actual numbers (e.g., "Pre: 2,400 spores/m³ → Post: 0 viable spores")

- Certification logos: IICRC, NORMI, EPA registration, any state licensing

### Process Section (fold 3)
- Three-step visual: Inspect → Dry Fog Treatment → Verify

- "Back in your home the same day" callout
- "No walls torn out, no reconstruction bills" callout

FAQ-style addressing the most common objections:
- "Does dry fogging really work?"
- "Is it safe for pets and children?"

| Remarketing / Display | Re-engage non-converting visitors | 10–15% |

### Consider Adding: Local Service Ads (LSAs)
- Separate from standard Google Ads — pay-per-lead model (~$50–150/lead)
- Requires Google Guaranteed badge (background check, insurance, license verification)
- LSAs appear **above** standard search ads
- Conversion rates: 30–45% vs. 18–28% for standard search
- Set up as soon as your review count supports it (Google requires a minimum review threshold)
